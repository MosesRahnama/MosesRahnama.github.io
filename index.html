Research

This is an ongoing research program on the structural limits of formal reasoning: the recurring situation where a system genuinely possesses a property, yet that property cannot be witnessed, oriented, or established by direct reasoning inside the system itself, so that crossing the gap requires a representation shift or an external mechanism. The work studies this boundary in its sharpest setting, a single minimal terminating system at the floor of computation, and traces the same phenomenon across termination, logical expressibility and incompleteness, the reasoning behavior of frontier AI systems, and confluence. It sits at the intersection of term rewriting, proof theory, and the empirical evaluation of machine reasoning, and every formal claim is mechanized and machine-checked in Lean 4, so the barriers are proven rather than asserted. The goal is to map where direct methods must fail, why they fail, and what it takes to move past them.

The Orientation Boundary for Step-Duplicating Recursors: Mechanized Impossibility, Escape, and Certification

arXiv2512.00081 Moses Rahnama Lean 4 · Term Rewriting · Termination Analysis

Formalizes the orientation boundary for first-order step-duplicating recursors. Proves that no measure in twelve formalized direct classes can strictly orient the duplicating step uniformly. Results are fully mechanized in Lean 4, constituting the first mechanized object-level barrier theorem for a fixed terminating system across multiple direct measure classes, proved without reductions or undecidability arguments. Also isolates and classifies escape routes within the direct universe.

Operational Inexpressibility at the Step-Duplicating Primitive Recursor Orientation Boundary

arXiv2604.22844 Moses Rahnama · Mina Analytics · April 2026 Term Rewriting · Proof Theory · Structural Barriers

Identifies a structural property of term-rewriting proof systems called operational inexpressibility: no derivation depends on a specified input dimension and also constrains the target question. Proves the canonical instance arises at the primitive recursion duplicator, where under any direct whole-term measure the recursor's mass profile coincides with that of a true circular reference. Connects the boundary to the Feferman–Beklemishev reflection family and recovers a structural identity with Gödel 1931 as a specialization.

Failing at the Floor: LLM Formal Reasoning Collapse on the Primitive Duplicating Recursor

Zenodo10.5281/zenodo.20034530 Moses Rahnama · Published May 5, 2026 · Version v4 LLM Benchmarking · Formal Reasoning · Termination Proving

Introduces the Primitive Recursor Test (PRT), a 27-model, 1,188-session benchmark over nine task families, graded on termination verdict, mathematical adequacy, and boundary-admissibility. On the KO7 kernel (TTT2-certified in 0.06 seconds), 94/324 (29.0%) of LLM sessions claimed the system does not terminate, only 3/324 retrieved the correct transformed-call witness, and 175/324 (54.0%) answered with a provably false method. Establishes a representation-shift bottleneck as a fundamental barrier to direct measure reasoning in frontier LLMs.

The Confluence-Preservation Boundary for Diagonal Identity Queries: Non-Left-Linearity, Signature Inexpressibility, and External Guarding

Zenodo10.5281/zenodo.20725654 Moses Rahnama · Mina Analytics · Published June 16, 2026 · Version v1 Term Rewriting · Confluence · Lean 4

Analyzes confluence failure in the KO7 witness calculus caused by a non-left-linear equality rule that creates unavoidable conflicts. Shows the resulting expressibility gap cannot be closed within the system's signature or kernel, and introduces an external guard mechanism that restores confluence. All claims are mechanized and verified in Lean 4.
